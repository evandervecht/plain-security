# Cyber insurance: four surprises at claim time

## intro {hold=1}
If our worst incident happens tomorrow, will the policy pay? [#four] Our insurance has four surprises at claim time.

## form
First, the application form. [#form] Every policy starts with a questionnaire, and someone in IT ticks yes in good faith. [#vpn] Two years later, an adjuster finds one VPN that never got multi-factor authentication. [#void] In many policies those answers are warranties, so the insurer may reduce the payout or void the cover.

## excluded
Second, the exclusions. [#war] Since 31 March 2023, Lloyd's requires its syndicates to exclude state-backed attacks from standalone cyber policies. [#patch] Known vulnerabilities left unpatched beyond a stated window are excluded too. [#fines] So are regulatory fines and contract penalties to customers. [#own] Each exclusion is exposure the board carries itself.

## limits
Third, the limits. [#head] The headline limit is not the number that matters. [#sub] Below it sit sub-limits for forensics and legal, and a waiting period before business interruption cover starts. [#cap] A serious incident uses up the forensics cap in the first days. Everything above the cap is ours.

## notify
Fourth, the phone call. [#hot] Most policies require immediate notice, and an approved panel of forensics and legal firms. [#own] On day one, IT calls the firm it already uses. [#late] Three days later, the insurer is told, and declines those invoices.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#ev] An evidence file behind every questionnaire answer. [#reg] An exclusion register, with an amount next to each line. [#lim] A limit test against a costed worst case. [#ins] The insurer's hotline on line one of the contact list. [#nis2] {NIS2|niss two} is clear that insurance does not replace the required risk measures.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#ask] Ask three questions at the next renewal. [#q1] What exactly is excluded? [#q2] How long is the waiting period? [#q3] Are the questionnaire answers still true today?

## outro {hold=2}
Insurance transfers some of the cost. [#keep] It never transfers the responsibility.
