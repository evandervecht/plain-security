# Endpoints: laptops, phones and the kitchen table

## intro {hold=1}
How many devices hold our company data, and how many could we wipe tonight? [#four] Four ways a device fails us.

## lost
First, a laptop is left in a taxi. [#disk] A login password protects the screen, not the disk. Without encryption, every file is readable in minutes. [#breach] That is usually a reportable {GDPR|G D P R} breach, with a 72-hour clock. [#fine] With encryption on, it is a hardware replacement and a form.

## personal
Second, company mail on a phone we have never seen. [#nowipe] We cannot enforce a screen lock, check encryption, or wipe it when it is lost. [#data] The downloaded files outlive the employee. [#bar] The point is not banning personal devices. Devices we can wipe, update and lock are welcome. The rest read mail in the browser.

## admin
Third, everyone is their own administrator. [#click] One click on a fake {PDF|P D F} tool installs it system-wide. [#off] It switches off the security tools and hides. [#cost] Removing admin rights costs a few weeks of helpdesk friction. [#keep] Keeping them costs the first incident.

## browser
Fourth, the browser is the new operating system. [#three] Updates wait weeks, a fake update banner delivers malware, and a resold extension reads every page. [#mfa] All of it happens inside the logged-in session, after {MFA|M F A}, and needs no admin rights.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#enc] Disk encryption and remote wipe. [#admin] No standing local admin, and an approved software catalogue. [#update] Auto-update within days, and an extension allow-list. [#cheap] These are settings in the device management we already pay for. [#nis2] {NIS2|niss two} names cyber hygiene, encryption and access control as risk measures.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#ask] Ask two questions. How many devices hold company data? [#wipe] And how many could we wipe tonight? [#gap] If the second number is smaller, that gap is the risk.

## outro {hold=2}
Four ways a device fails. [#keep] Count the devices we cannot wipe.
