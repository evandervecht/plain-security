# CADA or the framework? Same ladder, different rules

## intro {hold=1}
Which EU sovereignty yardstick applies to us? [#two] A framework we can use today, or a law that may bind tomorrow.

## two
First, two yardsticks. [#fw] The Commission's Cloud Sovereignty Framework is a procurement tool. It has five SEAL levels. [#cada] The Cloud and AI Development Act is a proposal. It has four assurance levels. [#bind] They would bind every public buyer in the EU. [#law] So one is guidance today, and the other is not yet law.

## how
Second, how the levels work. [#low] In the framework, the lowest of eight objectives sets the SEAL level. [#audit] Under the proposal, level 1 is self-assessed. Levels 2 to 4 need an independent audit. [#auth] A national authority recognises the result, and the service is listed in an EU repository.

## who
Third, who would have to comply. [#public] Public bodies would decide which activities count as public order, and use level 2, 3 or 4 for those. [#rest] Everything else needs level 1 at least. [#private] Private companies are not bound. [#prov] Providers choose recognition, but without it there is no public contract.

## examples
Fourth, the level follows the activity. [#muni] For municipalities, HR and finance would need level 1. [#hosp] For hospitals, patient care could need level 2 or 3. [#court] For courts, case files would need level 3. [#clock] If a service sits at the wrong level, the clock would be twelve months.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#map] An activity map, with a level for each activity. [#both] Suppliers scored on both ladders. [#clause] Level 2 clauses in contracts. [#exit] And a tested exit for critical services.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#three] Decide three things. [#acts] Which activities would count as public order. [#lvl] Which level we want from each critical supplier. [#vol] And whether to run the voluntary assessment before anyone asks.

## outro {hold=2}
One ladder to use today. [#keep] One proposal to watch.
