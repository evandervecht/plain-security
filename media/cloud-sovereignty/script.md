# Sovereign cloud: sovereign from whom?

## intro {hold=1}
Is our cloud sovereign? [#four] There is no yes or no answer. There is a level for each of four questions.

## level
First, which level. [#eight] The European Commission's Cloud Sovereignty Framework splits sovereign into eight objectives. [#scale] A provider scores from SEAL-0 to SEAL-4, and the lowest objective sets the level. [#two] For its own tender, the Commission asks for SEAL-2, data sovereignty. [#law] The framework is a procurement tool, not a law.

## law
Second, jurisdiction follows the company, not the server. [#cloud] The US CLOUD Act requires a US provider to hand over data in its control, inside or outside the United States. [#gdpr] GDPR Article 48 says a foreign order is enforceable in the EU only if an international agreement backs it. [#between] The provider sits between two laws.

## saas
Third, European infrastructure with SaaS outside it. [#sets] The SaaS sets our level, because that is where people work. [#leave] Content, identities and metadata usually leave. [#cond] Support access and AI processing leave under conditions we can negotiate. [#keys] Keys we hold stay with us.

## exit
Fourth, could we leave? [#stop] If a sanction, a court order or a bankruptcy ended the service tomorrow, how long until we work again? [#act] The EU Data Act, a law, caps the switching notice at two months. DORA requires exit strategies for critical services in finance. [#test] The simple test is to export everything, open it elsewhere, and time it.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#score] Score suppliers on the framework's eight objectives. [#own] Hold the keys ourselves. [#map] Map every flow that leaves each SaaS. [#exit2] Test one exit a year for each critical service.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#three] Decide three things. [#never] Which data must never be reachable by a foreign order. [#accept] Which level we accept for the rest. [#tested] And a tested exit for every critical service.

## outro {hold=2}
Sovereign is a level, not a yes or no. [#keep] Ask which level, for which data.
