# Quantum and AI: when two threats converge

## intro {hold=1}
Will the data we encrypt today still be safe in ten years? [#two] Quantum computers and AI speed each other up. Four risks follow.

## harvest
First, harvest now, decrypt later. [#store] A hostile party may be recording our encrypted traffic today. [#wait] It then waits for a quantum computer that can read it. [#ten] If the data still matters in ten years, the risk is today.

## shortcut
Second, AI may find shortcuts. [#pattern] AI can spot patterns in encrypted data and in implementations that people missed. [#notyet] This is not a known attack yet. It is a category of risk. [#test] We cannot assume our encryption is safe until we test it.

## both
Third, quantum and AI together. [#shor] A large quantum computer breaks RSA and ECC with Shor's algorithm, with no help from AI. [#aes] AES-256 stays strong. [#ai] AI speeds up quantum research, and finds implementation flaws faster.

## lag
Fourth, migration lag. [#aivd] The Dutch {AIVD|A I V D} says a useful quantum computer is possible from 2030. [#long] Migration is long, because it touches every system that holds secrets. [#late] Data encrypted today stays exposed if we switch late.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#inv] A cryptographic inventory. [#std] The NIST post-quantum standards, FIPS 203, 204 and 205. [#hyb] Hybrid post-quantum key exchange. [#hsm] Hardware security modules hold the keys. [#flex] And the ability to switch algorithms quickly.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#inv2] Ask for an inventory of where we use encryption, plus a plan, by the end of 2027. [#sens] Aim to protect the most sensitive data by 2030. Finish by 2035. [#rec] That is a suggested timeline, not a legal deadline.

## outro {hold=2}
The data we encrypt today will outlive both threats. [#keep] Start the migration now.
