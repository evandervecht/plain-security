# Insider threat: four people who already have the keys

## intro {hold=1}
What if someone who already has the keys takes what we cannot afford to lose? [#four] Insider threat comes in four kinds of people.

## leaver
First, the leaver. [#notice] In the notice period, a salesperson exports the customer list. [#open] Access stays fully open until the last day. [#normal] The export looks like normal work, and the damage shows months later.

## mistake
Second, the colleague who meant well. [#auto] Autocomplete picks the wrong address, and a salary overview goes outside. [#link] Or a folder is shared with anyone with the link. [#same] Nobody wanted harm, but it is the same breach, with the same regulator.

## admin
Third, the administrator nobody reviews. [#can] One person can export the customer database, [#log] create a spare account and clear the log. [#none] Then no evidence is left.

## pressure
Fourth, the employee under pressure. [#offer] Criminals offer a few thousand for a login. [#debt] Others are pushed with debt or threats. [#hard] A trusted account doing untrusted things is the hardest attack to detect.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#nis2] {NIS2|niss two} Article 21 names access control, human resources security and asset management. [#need] That means access limited to what the job needs, [#leave] access that shrinks when notice is given, [#eyes] a second person approving privileged actions, [#logs] and logs that administrators cannot erase. [#speak] Staff should feel safe to report being approached.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#five] Ask which five datasets would hurt most if one person walked out with them. [#many] Then ask how many people can reach each one today. [#answer] If nobody knows, that is the whole insider programme in one line.

## outro {hold=2}
Trust is not a control. [#keep] Limit access, and keep the evidence complete.
