# How much security is enough?

## intro {hold=1}
Every board asks it sooner or later. [#q] How much security is enough? [#five] Five questions turn it into something a board can govern.

## budget
First, the budget. [#median] The EU cybersecurity agency found that the median organisation spent about 9 percent of its IT budget on security. [#bench] That is a benchmark, not a target. [#risk] We suggest setting the risk appetite first, and then the budget.

## line
Second, the reporting line. [#ciso] Can the security lead say no to their boss? [#cio] Often they report to the CIO, who also owns the deadlines that security can delay. [#direct] We suggest a direct line to the board, or to its audit or risk committee.

## numbers
Third, five numbers every quarter. [#n1] Critical flaws fixed on time. [#n2] Admin accounts with strong MFA. [#n3] The last tested restore. [#n4] Time from alert to action, out of hours. [#n5] Overdue risks and findings. [#tol] Each one gets a tolerance, agreed in advance.

## review
Fourth, an outside view. [#own] The team should not mark its own homework. [#ia] DORA requires internal audit for financial entities. [#ext] Consider an external review after a serious incident, or before a merger.

## law
Fifth, what the law asks of the board itself. NIS2 Article 20 says management bodies [#d1] approve the security measures, [#d2] oversee them, [#d3] follow training, [#d4] and can be held liable.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#app] A written risk appetite, and a budget that follows it. [#dl] A security lead with a direct line. [#fn] Five numbers with tolerances. [#out] Outside review at set moments. [#min] The approval is in the minutes, and the training is on record.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#ask] Ask for three things. [#q1] The written risk appetite. [#q2] Proof that our security lead can reach us without permission. [#q3] The minuted approval of the measures.

## outro {hold=2}
Enough security is enough to stay within the risk the board agreed to accept. [#rec] Write it down, and record it.
