# Ransomware: four steps to a standstill

## intro {hold=1}
How does a stolen login become weeks of downtime? [#four] Ransomware takes four steps.

## entry
First, someone walks in through the front door. [#vpn] A {VPN|V P N} with no second factor, or an unpatched edge device. [#quiet] They sign in like an employee, so no alarm fires. [#fix] Consider a second factor that resists phishing, and a small, patched edge.

## spread
Second, one laptop becomes the whole company. [#flat] On a flat network with shared admin accounts, the attacker reaches domain admin. [#all] Then everything is reachable. [#seg] Consider segmentation, tiered admin and endpoint detection.

## steal
Third, they copy everything first. [#copy] The night before, they copy the data and encrypt later. [#leak] A leak site threatens to publish the data unless we pay. [#breach] That makes it a data breach, not just downtime. [#egress] Consider egress monitoring and least-privilege data access.

## backups
Fourth, the backups go first. [#friday] At two on a Friday night, the attacker deletes the online backup. [#ransom] Then the ransom is demanded. [#immutable] Consider an immutable offline copy, and a tested full restore.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#two] Two numbers decide everything. How many days can we be down, and how much data can we lose? [#price] Every control is priced against them. [#pay] Decide now, in calm, whether we would ever pay. Paying a sanctioned group can itself be unlawful. [#police] Report to the police either way, and check No More Ransom for a free decryptor.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#ask] Ask the CIO for four numbers. [#a] External logins without a second factor, and days to patch a critical flaw. [#b] The date of the last full restore test, and the share of endpoints with detection.

## outro {hold=2}
Four steps to a standstill. [#keep] Break the chain, and test the restore.
