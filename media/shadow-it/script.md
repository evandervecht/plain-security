# Shadow IT: the data you don't know you have

## intro {hold=1}
How many online tools hold our company data, and how many can IT name? [#gap] If the second number is smaller, that gap is the risk. [#four] It opens in four ways.

## signup
First, a team signs up with a credit card. [#card] Sales finds a better lead tool, and uploads the customer list to get started. [#ask] Nobody asks IT, because asking IT is slow. [#none] We now store customer data with a supplier we have no contract with. [#gdpr] Under {GDPR|G D P R}, we are still responsible.

## personal
Second, work happens on personal accounts. [#deck] A key presentation is finished on a home laptop. A contract is forwarded to a personal mailbox. [#none] There is no second factor, no backup, and no way to wipe a lost device. [#leave] When that person leaves, the data leaves too.

## forgotten
Third, the subscription nobody remembers. [#pilot] A pilot that never became a project still holds the data that was uploaded. [#bill] It still bills the card every month. [#admin] And its administrator may have left the company two years ago.

## consent
Fourth, one click hands an app the keys to the mailbox. [#allow] A scheduling assistant asks to read the calendar, and in the same dialog, all mail and all files. [#click] The employee clicks Allow. [#survive] That access survives a password reset, and is not stopped by the second factor.

## good {hold=1.2}
What good looks like. [#sso] Single sign-on makes the company login the only door. [#disc] Discovery from expense reports and login logs shows which tools are really in use. [#consent] Admin consent means employees cannot grant mail access on their own. [#nis2] {NIS2|N I S two} asks for an inventory of assets and suppliers, and security across the supply chain.

## first {hold=1.2}
A suggested first step. [#count] Ask how many online tools hold company data today. [#name] Then ask how many of them IT can name. [#reg] One supplier register, with an owner for each tool. [#rule] No owner, no tool.

## outro {hold=2}
You cannot protect data you do not know you have. [#see] Bring it back into view.
